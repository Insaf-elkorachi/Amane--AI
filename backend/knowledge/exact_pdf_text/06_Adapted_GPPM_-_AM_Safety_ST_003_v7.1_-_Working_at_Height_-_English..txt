1. Scope 
1.1. Group Companies will, as a minimum, follow the prevailing local regulations for elevated 
work where there is a risk of falling. Where this ArcelorMittal standard is more demanding, 
then it will apply. 
1.2. In any case, fall prevention or protection shall be used for elevated work above 1.8 meters 
(6 feet). However, for any task where the risk assessment highlights a danger of falling, 
proper actions must be taken, even if the distance one can fall is less than 1,8 m.
2. Definitions 
2.1.  Fall prevention. Wherever practical, a safe working area must be provided by means of  
work platforms or scaffolds. Fall Prevention standards are set out below. 
2.2.  Fall protection. In all other cases, Fall Protection must be used. This includes situations in 
which work is being carried out from an elevated work pla tform or man-lift/cherry picker, 
except work of mi nor extend on portable ladders.  Fall Protection standards are set out 
below. 
2.3. Ladders. 
2.3.1. portable ladders: 
see annex A1 to this standard.
2.3.2. fix ladders: 
see annex A2 to this standard

NB:  following accidents that incl u de a fatality in 2022, r ope and cable  ladders ( two long ropes 
or flexible cables,  connected by short crosspieces (rungs ) , typically made of wood or metal, used 
as a ladder ) are banned. They cannot be used within our premises, nor by us nor by any 
contractor or subcontractor.  For the exceptional cases where no alternative would be technically 
possible, it is mandatory to use simultaneously and continuously a self-retracting lifeline.  
2.4. Restricted access. Where overhead work is being conducted, there must be demarcation 
to restrict access around the work area to protect ot hers below from falling objects and to 
prevent the platforms / scaffolds or other to be hit by any form of “traffic”.
2.5. Dock - a structure extending alongshore or out from the shore into a body of water, to 
which ore boats, barges etc. may be moored.

3. Fall Prevention 
3.1.  Work platforms and scaffolds must have complete floors, guardrails and toe-boards. Safe 
access and egress must be provided. 
3.2.  A person must be designated to control the work platform, scissor lift or man-lift (“the 
basket”),  which is trained and competent to do so and qualified as required under local 
regulations.
3.3.  Every person in the ‘basket’ must be secured at all ti mes with proper Fall Protection  
equipment. When exiting the basket, Fall Protection must be used during transition unless 
otherwise protected. There must be systems in place to prevent tools and equipment from 
falling. 
3.4.  There shall be a system for ensuring the design, construction, certification, maintenance 
and inspection of elevated work platforms (including scaffolds) and man-lifts. 
4. 
Fall Protection 
4.1. Any person that is required to use fall protection must be trained and competent.
4.2. A person will be subjected to Fall Protection if s/he is secured with an approved full body 
harness, shock absorbing lanyard (where the potential to fall is greater than 4 meters) or 
short restraining lanyards (where the potential to fall is less than 4 meters), self-locking 
snap hooks (or carabineer type rings) and secure anchorage points. Safety belts are not 
adequate fall protection mechanisms and do not comply with this standard. 
Where fall protection equipment is required – before work begins ,  a pre-task H&S plan, 
checklist or pre task HIRA, Job Safety Analysis or Toolbox Talk must:
o
Be completed with all workers involved in the Work at Height tasks (to ensure 
awareness and alignment of the planned safety measures) 
o
Specifically state the fall protection / prevention equipment to be used for all tasks to 
be performed 
o
Specify the anchoring system / points to be used 
o
Be reviewed and signed by all workers and their supervisor.
4. 3 . Where movement beyond the reach of a single lanyard is required, a risk assessment must 
be carried out to determine the need for  lifelines and double lanyards. Where double 
lanyards are required, the person must ensure that at least one is attached all the time.

Anchorage points  and lifelines  must, where practical, be above the head of the worker, and 
must ensure that in the event of a fall the worker will neither swing into a hazard nor touch 
the ground.  In any case, the fall factor must be estimated and taken into account to check 
that the person is effectively protected:

anchorage point(s) and lifeline will resist, 

user will not hit the ground or another obstacle during the fall, and 

user ‘s body will not be submitted to a dynamic force greater than 6 kN).
4.4.1.  There must be a system for ensuring that anchorage  points  and lifelines  are 
designed ,  approved , and regularly verified  by a competent person to ensure that 
they are secure and can take the required load. In case of absence of detailed 
specific requirements in the local legislation, EN 353 or equivalent should be 
followed.  A competent person is one who has received appropriate training and is 
suitably certified / qualified by local regulatory schemes. 
4.4.2. Installation and maintenance/repairs of the anchorage points and lifelines must:
only be conducted by a competent person, and strictly in accordance with the 
equipment technical specifications.
The HIRA and SOP for the installation of new anchorage points and lifelines must 
provide means to protect the person(s) installing the device from fall from height 
risks (e.g. cherry picker, mobile lifting platforms, man basket,  temporary lifelines, 
 …).
4.5. There must be a system for ensuring that Fall Protection equipment is: 
4.5.1. Tested and certified for use.
4.5.2. Inspected by the user before use and, 
4.5.3.  Destroyed following a fall or where inspection has shown evidence of excessive 
wear or mechanical malfunction. 
4.6. There must be  an  emergency rescue procedure for fall victims for each job. Emergency 
response plans must be regularly practiced. 
4.7. Roofing activities  and floor construction/repair activities (installing, assembling or replacing 
gratings or floor plates)
All roofing activities (repairs, replacement, construction, cleaning, …),  and all activities 
involving a floor construction or repair (when there is a risk of floor collapse or when there 
is a floor opening),  including all movements associated with these activities between the 
roof /  floor access point and the place of work (including the supply and removal of material 
and tools) should be firstly secured by the mean of fall preventing collective solutions (e.g. 
safe platforms and walkways fitted with handrails).
In addition to the above, fall protecting collective solutions can include the use of safety 
nets capable of arresting the fall of people and/or equipment when work is being carried

For installing roof panels on new buildings (new constructions): the usage of safety nets is

When roofing/ flooring activities involve the opening and/or replacement/repair of some 
roof/ floor panels, including skylight panels, or when collective solutions are not fully 
protecting the activities, they must be continuously monitored and controlled by an 
individual competent person (often called “Red Scorpion”, can be internal or third party) not 
involved  in the activity and having the authority to intervene in case of any deviation 
observed. This permanent monitoring can be done through cameras or physical presence 
at height. In 1 st   case, the monitoring person must have continuous verbal contact with 
someone on the roof, so he can intervene if he observes an unsafe act or situation. The  
Red Scorpion ’s  mission in not to supervise the job, but to provide continuous safety 
auditing of the controls foreseen by the HIRA. He has to provide a daily written report 
about his activity.  In case of a person unclipped, even temporarily, whereas the HIRA 
states he should be clipped, the duty of the “Red Scorpion” person is to have  the  works
stopped  immediately  and  all  concerned  people  evacuated  safely  without  delay. The 
violator must have his/her work permit / access badge removed immediately (he/she is not 
allowed to finish any work, except if this would lead to higher risks, which has to be formally 
validated and authorized by the plant or project director) and be accompanied to the exit of 
the premise. This must be very clear in the “Red Scorpion” person’s mission contract.
Map detailing the movements on the roof/floor: for all activities, during the planning of 
the work, a detailed map of the roof/floor must be drawn for each phase of the works.

This map must show the exact detailed circulation walkways, around the working 
place, and from access/egress points to the working place, for people, tools and 
materials. 

A color code should be used to explain where being clipped to a lifeline is 
mandatory (and which lifeline) and where it is not mandatory (areas secured thanks
to robust floor + railings). 

The map will show the range accessible from each lifeline, taking into account the 
length of the lanyards. 

A check will be made to check that the movements necessary to perform the works,
bring and evacuate tools and materials, are feasible. 

Points where people have to change from on lifeline to another will be highlighted. 

The map will also clearly identify the places where storing materials and tools is 
allowed, and the maximum weight allowed. This maximum weight has to be 
calculated by a competent person, after checking the actual condition of the 
structure.

This map will be part of the HIRA and will have to be signed each time there is a 
change and at least once a day, before accessing the roof/floor, by each of the 
person. When a shift change occurs, or when there is a work interruption, whereas 
some roof panel or floor grating/plate have been positioned but have not yet been 
secured/fixed, the map has to be updated with this information.

4.8. Over, On, or Near Water along Docks.
4.8.1. All individuals over, on or near water along docks and within 3 meters (10 feet)
of the water, must wear “Approved life jackets” or “buoyant work vests”.
4.8.2. This includes those individuals boarding or de-boarding a barge, ore boat, etc. 
4.8.3. This includes those individuals operating mobile equipment.
4.8.4. Unprotected boundaries of  3 meters (10 feet)  from the water dock edge must 
be demarcated.
4.8.5. Signs stating “Approved life jackets or buoyant work vests must be worn” must 
be posted along the demarcated areas.
4.8.6. Where activities that involve being over, on or near water are being carried out 
during the hours of darkness, adequate lighting must be provided and 
maintained.  ( Follow the national legal/official recommendations. As guidelines  
value 50 Lux, can be considered as safe)  
4.8.7. When ladders or gangways are used to board or disembark a motorized 
vessel, safety nets must be installed under the ladder or gangway.
4.8.8. A gangway with railings  or fixed ladder  must be utilized when accessing 
barges.
