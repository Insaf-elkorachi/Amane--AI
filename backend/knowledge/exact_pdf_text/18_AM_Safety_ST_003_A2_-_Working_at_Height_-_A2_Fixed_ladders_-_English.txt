1. Definitions:  
 
A fixed ladder is a vertical ladder mounted permanently to a structure. These ladders are 
primarily used to access roofs or other structures for industrial purposes. The fix ladder(s) 
should be designed with two stiles. In exceptional cases (e.g., insufficient space), ladder(s) with 
one central stile may be foreseen.  
 
 
2. Requirements 
 
Fixed ladders should comply with local legal regulations, as well as the below requirements. In 
case of conflict, the local regulations prevail.  
 
2.1 General requirements applicable to all fixed ladders  
• 
The use of fixed ladders is only permitted if the installation of a staircase is impossible or 
not reasonable considering the very low frequency of use.  
• 
Certification of the ladder’s carrying capacity should be displayed on site. 
 
2.2 Requirements specific to fixed ladders of lengths between 3000 and 5000 mm 
• 
for fixed ladders between 3 m and 5 m, it is mandatory that users clip alternatively on ladder 
rungs with their double lanyard. This requirement must be indicated with a sign at the foot of 
the ladder. 
 
2.3 Requirements specific to fixed ladders with overall length ≥ 5000 mm  
• 
Ground access to the vertical ladder must be controlled.  
• 
User must have received a specific training for using fixed ladders.  
• 
User must wear a safety harness.

And one of the following features:  
 
• 
Option A (preferred): a ladder-mounted fall arresting system (FAS), with a rail or cable 
system, to which the user is connected through his frontal D-ring (safety harness must have 
a frontal D-ring, preferably sternal). In case the climber uses a lanyard to connect to the rail 
trolley or cable sleeve, it must be appropriate and short (length < 0.5 m) to minimize the 
fall factor, always refer to the FAS manufacturer for selection and proper use. User must 
carry an additional lanyard to perform transition between vertical axis (ladder) to the 
anchor point’(s) at the ladder’s entrance/exit. FAS must be inspected by a competent 
person on a yearly basis or less.   
A ladder cage by itself (alone), is not considered as a FAS.

Consideration on ladder cages: there has been wide recognition that cages do little to protect the worker 
when they fall since it’s the worker’s body that arrests the fall. Cages lead to workers getting tangled up 
in them after a fall, and they can make rescues quite difficult. They can mislead users to wrongly believe 
that they will be protected from falling or make their fall less severe, which there is no evidence of. 
 
 
Reminders:  
• 
for ladders shorter than 3m, if the person is above 1.8 m, there must be a fall protection 
or prevention system, such as e.g., the ones described in options A, B, C above.  
 
• 
In all situations where a lanyard with shock absorbing system is 
used, always check the manufacture’s report regarding fall factor 
vs. shock absorbing system activation, in order to make sure the 
available clearance is enough prevent hitting the ground or other 
obstacles if the fall would happen. 
 
 
3. Inventory & regular inspections: 
 
 
An inventory and identification of all fix ladders must be carried out including the determination 
of the ladder owner as well as the inspection status. 
A competent person must inspect fix ladders for visible defects annually and after any incident 
that could affect their safe use.  
Inspection check lists or specific documents need to be recorded and kept at minimum over 2 
inspection periods.   
 
 
4. Training Requirements 
 
 
Own employees as well as employees from service providers regularly involved in working at 
height jobs must be trained to the appropriate use. Regular retraining must be considered.
