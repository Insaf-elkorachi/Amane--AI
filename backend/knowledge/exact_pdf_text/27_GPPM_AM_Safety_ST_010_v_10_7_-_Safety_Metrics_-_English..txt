1. Purpose
The purpose of this procedure is to define the ArcelorMittal Safety Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).
It does not prevent ArcelorMittal companies and plants from tracking, on top of the ArcelorMittal official 
KPIs, other local performance indicators in accordance with local laws.
2. Scope
The KPIs are computed for  own  personnel  “ OP ”  as well as  service providers  “SP”  ( companies contracted 
directly or indirectly by ArcelorMittal  to perform work on a site owned or controlled by ArcelorMittal ) .  T his 
also includes the transporting companies, for the part of their activities occurring on ArcelorMittal sites (i.e., 
loading, unloading and transport within ArcelorMittal sites).
A t this time  “ Value Chain Workers ” (CSRD  future requirement)  are not in the scope  of reporting , p ending  
precision from CSRD on definition. 
L ocal HR policies will dictate whether a person  is  own personnel “OP” or service provider “SP” .   If you have 
any questions on this topic, please contact your local HR department for clarification.
NB:  when a contract ing company  is  contractually  renting  or  leasing  from  ArcelorMittal a  ringfenced  area,  in 
which it  perfor ms its own  activities ( e.g., repair shop),  with  no  ArcelorMittal  activity  in  this  area, then an 
accident happening inside this area is considered as outside of ArcelorMittal site, and therefore not 
consolidated in ArcelorMittal statistics, except if legal authorities conclude that ArcelorMittal responsibility is 
involved in this accident.  Presence of a pre-existing formal renting or leasing land contract, as well as the 
fencing of the area is mandatory.
The KPIs  concern  all employees  without distinction,  permanent  or  temporary  ( e.g.,  contract limited in time, 
temporary job, holiday job, student job, trainee purpose, etc.)
To be taken into account, an injury must be simultaneously:

Caused by a sudden, single instantaneous event. 

Caused by the work and not the result of any pre-existing underlying medical conditions with a  history of  
symptoms – this is to be determined only by medical professional.

Reported on a timely basis – is there connection between the symptoms and the injury date – this is to be 
determined only by a medical professional.

Classified as work related by local authorities
For external reporting, the scope is the one of legal entities structure (BPM consolidation structure), used for
financial consolidation purpose.

3.2. Fatality
Is a n  incident caused by  work that occurs on company property or while travelling  for company business  and  
which results in a fatality.
3.3. Lost Time Injury (LTI)
Is a work-related  injury that results in the loss of at least one full working day (beyond the da y of the injury).  
So, if there is any absence beyond the day of the injury occurring, the consequence of the incident is 
automatically an LTI. This is independent  of the fact that the Physician  prescribed staying at home or doing 
adapted work: reality is to be used for accounting.
The treating Physician will judge whether the injured employee is able to do adapted work or whether a 
work stoppage is needed. 
If, in the opinion of the Company’s Physician, the injured employee is able to perform restricted work 
without undue hardship or compromising the recovery process and the injured employee consents to this, it 
is the responsibility of the Company’s Physician to liaise with the treating Physician regarding suitable duties . 
If the treating Physician is in agreement, he may then issue an amended sick note.

3.4. Commuting to/from home incidents
Any  inci dent on the public road during trip from home to the workplace or from the workplace back home 
with any type of vehicle or on foot. They are not considered as work related. 
They do not include i ncidents  occurring  inside the site or during business travel  (business travel covers all the 
time from leaving home or normal working place, wherever the business trip is starting, till getting back 
home or to normal place of work), which are considered workplace accidents.
3.5. Reoccurring Injury (Relapse)
A relapse of an earlier injury ( i.e., person is absent a second, third, … time due to the same event) is not 
counted as a new LTI (so not included in frequency rate a second, third, … time) but all days are counted as 
lost days (so ALL lost days of EACH absence to be included in severity rate). 
However, if the initial incident was a Medical Aid or First Aid and thus did not result in the loss of time at 
start and the reoccurrence does result in days lost then the injury is to be counted as a lost time injury (i.e. 
put in the LTI category and take out of the Medical Aid or First Aid category, thus avoiding the same incident 
would be counted twice) and the lost days are to be counted in the severity metric.
3.6. Restricted Work Injury (also called ‘Adapted Work’) (RW) 
An injury  which results in no time lost but the employee is not able to perform  ALL of his/her normal pre- 
injury work functions or is assigned to suitable other modified work until he/she can perform all his/her pre- 
injury work tasks without restrictions.  Restrictions and the ability to return to pre-injury tasks are to be 
determined by a Physician.
To be clear: 

The task the person is doing must be a MEANINGFUL task.

I f restricted work is offered, but the injured stays at home, the inju ry is to be counted as an LTI, t here is no 
room for interpretation: if the person is not at work, it is an LTI in all cases. 
3.7. Medical Aid Injury
A work injury  which is  requiring treatment by a  Physician, and which is beyond the scope of first aid. 
(Physician’s Assistants (PA) and Nurse Practitioners (NP) are to be considered Physicians for this document 
since they practice under the license and direction of a Physician.) 
Some examples include, but are not limited to: 
a) Application of Sutures 
b) Removal of Foreign Bodies Embedded in Eye (invasive procedure) 
c) Positive X-Ray Diagnosis (fractures, dislocation, disc injury etc) 
d) Loss of Consciousness due to an injury or exposure in the work environment 
e) Use of prescription medicine. 
However, the consequences are such that normal work is done, without any loss of working day after the day 
of the occurrence.
From 01.01.2014 the reporting of Medical Aid Injuries and the upload in SaRA is mandatory.

3.8. First Aid (i.e., not to be declared to local authorities) 
An injury  which do es not require trea tment by a medical professional and the consequences are such that 
normal work is done, without any loss of working day after the day of the occurrence. 
3.9. Near Hits
Any  event  which effectively occurred, but  which (fortunately) did not create any harm to  people but may do 
in any subsequent occurrence. 
Example:

A piece of equipment or a load fell from a crane but did not hit anybody.

A car drove through a red light and a pedestrian could jump away just in time to avoid being hit.

B ecause a lock out was not properly done, the control room operator started a pump that was being 
maintained, but fortunately the maintenance man had just left to find an appropriate tool to do the job.
3.10.
Unsafe situation
Any situation  which , sooner or later, may lead to an incident inflicting harm to one or more persons or 
leading to material damage. 
Example:

A lifting cable found damaged but still in use.

A traffic light has a defective red light.

Part of a hand railing is missing or broken and as a consequence a fall from height is possible.

Material dragging around on the floor, leading to a risk of trips and falls.
3.11.
Unsafe acts
Any action which does endanger the person him/herself and/or the people working around.
Example:

W orking at heights ( e.g.,  on a roof) without  respect of the appropriate  controls  (to be  defined by  the 
related HIRA (Hazard Identification and Risks Assessment)).

Although the traffic light is red, someone still quickly crosses the street.

Someone enters a confined space without gas detection equipment.

Starting a job without proper lock out being in place nor testing it before starting the job.
Unsafe acts and unsafe situations are latent conditions for accidents. 
3.12.
Potential Serious Injury and Fatality (PSIF)
It’s an event, act or situation for which consequences or  worst  potential consequences  are  or could have 
been a fatality or a permanent  severe  disability such  as: loss of a hand, loss of a limb, loss of an eye, 
paraplegia, tetraplegia. 
Examples:

Someone got crushed between a fix point and a mobile equipment or load (event)

Someone fell from > 1.8 m (event)

Something heavy fell in an area where people are used to work (event)

Someone entering an area or putting part of his body inside a machine, without isolation or, if 
isolation not possible, without strong controls deeply thought in a specific HIRA-lite (unsafe act)

3.13.
Worked hours
The number of hours actually worked or an estimation if the actual figure not available (Contractor’s 
activities, …). The hours actually worked and those regarded as such include the time spent for training or 
other work required activities but does not take into account holidays or other days off.
3.14.
Days lost (Severity Rate, Sr)
These are all days lost following an injury; in other words, one week’s absence is equivalent to 7 days lost 
(i.e., including scheduled working days and weekends or equivalent planned resting and recuperation days).
In the case of an immediate fatality no days are to be counted. If a fatality is not instantaneous, but death 
occurs a number of days or months after the incident, then the days lost between the occurrence of the 
injury and the day the person passes away, are to be counted in the normal #Lost Days field.  No additional 
tracking of the lost days, up until the fatality, will be reported. Country specific legislation requiring a 
calculation of additional days lost due to a fatality are NOT included in SaRA but must be recorded and 
reported locally.
The maximum days lost for each injury is limited to 12 months (365 days). However, if a person leaves the 
company before the end of this period, only the days from the event to the leaving date (due to job finished, 
retirement, …) are to be counted. 
(Example, a person is injured 1 week before retirement, and dies 8 months after retirement, the lost days 
counted would be 7, the days until retirement.) It is not expected to have any information on a person who 
has left the company for any reason.
A relapse of an earlier Lost Time injury is not counted as a new LTI, but the days lost are counted (i.e., one-
time count for Fr but ALL lost days to be counted in Sr), independent of when the relapse is taken place.

6. Risk Definitions as used in REX and correspondence with WSA (World Steel Association) “Causes of 
incidents” definitions (source = WSA report 2021, used in 2022)
Burnt by fire (term used in REX) / Fire (WSA)
Any incident resulting from the combustion of materials and propagation of the flames causing damage 
to people, installation and the environment.
Burnt by hot substance (other than liquid metal) / Hot Substances (WSA)
Incident caused by exposure to any type of hot material, equipment, surface, steam or water. If the injury 
is caused by hot metal, the injury is categorized into that cause.
e.g., burns caused by hot equipment in contact with skin
Burnt by liquid metal or slag / Hot Metal (WSA)
Incidents caused by hot or liquid metal. Main risks are heat radiation, splashing, scalding from hot metal
e.g., projection of hot metal on a person; burn by radiation of hot metal
Burnt or injured by explosion or blast / Explosion (WSA)
A release of energy that causes a pressure discontinuity or blast wave (e.g., detonations, deflagrations, 
and rapid releases of high pressure caused by rupture of equipment or piping).
e.g., water in liquid steel, leak of oxygen, generation and leak of hydrogen or leak of CO or blast furnace 
gas can drive to an explosion
Burnt or intoxicated by chemical or ionizing radiations / Exposure to Chemicals (WSA)
Incident caused by contact or exposure to hazardous chemical substance.
If the injury is caused by breathing toxic gas, the injury is categorized into cause gassing / asphyxiation.
e.g., acid burn, chemical spill, allergic reaction
Crushed by collapsing structure or building / Structural Failure (WSA)
Any incident resulting from a failure of the structure of a building, machinery or equipment
e.g., deficient scaffolding, roof or wall panels or structural frames, weather conditions or lack of 
maintenance may deteriorate surface, constructive defect of machinery
Crushed by moving machinery / Moving Machinery (WSA)
Incident caused by any component of machinery or equipment that can move by any energy source 
(electrical, steam, hydraulic, pneumatic, heat, wind, product such as strip being pulled by other 
equipment), by remote control or by gravity.
e.g., crushed by the movement of a shaft rotated by an operator in a remote cabin, unexpected start of 
an un-isolated motor or engine, unexpected start of a conveyor, trapped between the belt and the roll of 
a conveyor, crushed by the movement of a cover or table of a machine operated by hydraulic or 
pneumatic cylinder. 
 
Clarification on the WSA definition: “Moving machinery” has to be understood here as a machine that is 
fixed to the ground, but that has movable parts/components.

Gassed asphyxiated / Gassing/Asphyxiation (WSA)
Incident in any area where gas can accumulate or be trapped, and the air does not sustain or support 
life.
- “gassing” when the breathing air contents a toxic gas.
e.g., gassing due to a rate of carbon monoxide (CO) in the breathing air above the threshold limit 
depending on the duration of the exposition to the CO
- “asphyxiation” occurs when the oxygen rate in the breathing air decreases below 19.5%
e.g., asphyxiation due to the presence of carbon dioxide (CO2), nitrogen (N2), argon (Ar) or any other 
gas taking the place of the oxygen in the breathing air
Hit by projected substance or object (other than in eye) / no equivalent category in WSA
Injury caused by a substance or object that has been thrown or projected. Examples would include 
objects breaking under tension or pressure, 
e.g., hoist straps or hooks, supports under load, etc.
Hit by projected substance or object in eye / Object in the Eye (WSA)
Incident caused by something that enters the eye from outside the body.
e.g., steel sliver in the eye, dust in the eye
Hit/crushed by falling object or material / Falling Object (WSA)
An object falling on a person for any reason, also objects that can be released sideways or upwards are 
considered.
e.g., tool falling from a scaffold, load falling from a crane, product falling due the collapse of a pile of 
products, something stored vertically falling or sliding down, building components broken during storm, 
or broken by snow, ice, or even hail, snow or ice blocks.
Offsite road / Off site road/vehicle (WSA)
Incident on the public road with any type of vehicle or on foot to and from the workplace. Includes 
business travel.
e.g., sales or marketing people injured on the road during the working time, employees driving to an 
externally organized training session
Others / Other (WSA)
Incident is caused by a reason which is not listed. In case there are several reasons behind the incident, 
the most suitable is chosen.
Pinched, cut / no equivalent category in WSA
Small injuries resulting in a pinch or cut not covered under one of the other more descriptive categories. 
This could perhaps fall under the Manual Tasks and Tools WSA category, but for the moment, we make 
no such connection.
